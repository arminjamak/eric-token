# Morsiri — a 9-note scale with a 12/5 period, and a piece written in it

Morsiri is a scale that repeats not at the octave but at **12/5** (an octave plus a just
minor third, 1515.6 c). It has nine notes per period, two step sizes, a strong "fifth"
pillar, a leading tone that resolves upward into the tonic, and one deliberately strange
note. The piece `morsiri.scd` (SuperCollider, ~66 s) is written entirely in it, with a synth
whose overtones lie on the scale rather than on the harmonic series.

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `morsiri.scd` | the piece: tuning, matched-spectrum synth, score, playback / offline render |
| `morsiri.mp3` | offline render of the piece (made with the non-realtime server) |
| `scale_search.py` | exhaustive search over periods and generators against the brief's rules |
| `candidates.py` | full evaluation of the three shortlisted scales + known-system subset test |
| `timbre.py` | the matched spectrum and its Sethares roughness curve |
| `verify_audio.py` | checks the render: level, duration, spectral peaks vs the tuning table |

## Part 1 — Designing the scale

### Method

Rather than guess, I searched. `scale_search.py` sweeps ten candidate periods between
1.9 and 3.2 (3/1, 5/2, 7/3, 8/3, 9/4, 11/4, 12/5, 11/5, 13/5, φ+1) and every generator from
40 c to half the period in 0.5 c steps, keeps only the moment-of-symmetry sizes between 5
and 9 notes, and scores each mode against the brief:

- generator not within 15 c of 3/2, 4/3, 5/4, 6/5 or any multiple of 100 c;
- exactly two step sizes with 1.4 ≤ L/s ≤ 2.6, each used at least twice (a chain of eight
  equal steps plus one gap is technically an MOS but gives no degree a distinct role);
- at least four intervals from the tonic within 8 c of a just ratio with n·d ≤ 100, at
  least two of them ratios 12-TET misses by more than 15 c;
- at least 60 % of the intervals more than 15 c from every 12-TET interval;
- exactly one interval 20–40 c from anything familiar (12-TET or a simple ratio), none
  further than 40 c;
- a small step s adjacent to the tonic.

Two filters were added after looking at the first results. The tritave hits generated by
~439 c turned out to be Bohlen-Pierce Lambda in disguise (439 c is 9/7), so generators
within 25 c of 9/7 are rejected for the 3/1 period. And many "spicy" intervals were a
fifth or an octave 30-odd cents flat, which an untrained ear hears as *wrong*, not as
*colour*; so a perceptual filter rejects any interval 9–25 c from 2/1, 3/2 or 4/3, and
requires the spicy interval to be at least 60 c away from those anchors.

### The three candidates

| | **A — neutral-third chain** | **B — undecimal heptatonic** | **C — tritave octatonic** |
|---|---|---|---|
| period | 12/5 (1515.6 c) | 11/4 (1751.3 c) | 3/1 (1902.0 c) |
| generator | 349.4 c | 778.0 c (≈ 11/7) | 737.0 c |
| notes / pattern | 9, LsLsLsLss | 7, LsssLss | 8, LLsLLsLs |
| L, s, L/s | 231.2, 118.2, **1.96** | 387.4, 195.3, 1.98 | 309.0, 118.9, 2.60 |
| consonances within 8 c | **8** (6 alien to 12-TET) | 6 (5) | 6 (5) |
| novelty (>15 c from 12-TET) | 78 % | 86 % | 75 % |
| pillar | 3/2 at 698.7 c | 11/7 at 778 c (a fifth 76 c sharp) | 10/7 at 618 c |
| leading tone | s = 118 c (≈ 15/14) | s = 195 c (a whole tone) | s = 119 c |
| spicy interval | 1279 c: 21 c from 1300, 59 c from 13/6, 79 c from 2/1 | 1556 c: a 5/2 that is 30 c flat | 737 c: a 3/2 that is 35 c sharp |
| near-misses (9–25 c off an anchor) | none | none | 1355 c is 10 c off 11/5: neither consonant nor novel |

**Why A wins.** It has the most consonances, and the best *structure*: the pillar is a real
3/2, the leading tone is a diatonic-sized semitone (≈15/14) that lands on the tonic, and the
pillar chord 1 : 3/2 : 9/4 contains the leading tone, so cadences work exactly the way
dominant → tonic does. Its spicy note sits in the one region where a strange pitch reads as
expression rather than error: three-quarters of a semitone above where an octave would be,
in a scale that has no octave at all.

**Why not B.** Only seven notes, and the small step is a whole tone, so there is no
semitone-like pull into the tonic. Its pillar, 11/7, is a fifth 76 c sharp; its spicy note
is a flat 5/2. Both are heard as mistuned versions of familiar intervals. Great chord
(4 : 5 : 7 is in the scale), weak melody.

**Why not C.** Fails the perceptual test outright: the "spicy" interval is a fifth 35 c
sharp (a wolf), and 1355 c is 10 c off 11/5, close enough to sound like a wrong note and too
far to sound like a tuned one. L/s is also at the 2.6 ceiling.

### The chosen scale: Morsiri

**Period** 12/5 = 1515.64 c. Why this period: of the ten periods searched it produced the
scale with the most consonances and the cleanest cadence structure, and it avoids the two
traps of the range. 3/1 is Bohlen-Pierce territory, and 5/2 scales tend to contain a 2/1
and then sound like octave scales with a wrong top note. 12/5 is an octave plus a just
minor third, so when a phrase repeats a period higher it is heard as a *lift*, a modulation
up a minor tenth, rather than as the same thing again. Its own consonance is modest
(n·d = 60) but exact, and the matched timbre makes it the smoothest interval in the system
(see Timbre).

**Generator** g = (96/35)^(1/5) = **349.36 c**, a tempered neutral third. The fifth root is
chosen so that five generators span exactly one period plus 8/7; this makes the large step
exactly 8/7 and keeps every other target ratio within 6.5 c. Two generators make the pillar
(698.7 c, a 3/2 that is 3.2 c narrow), four make 9/4.

The scale is two chains of neutral thirds a septimal whole tone apart:
{1, g, g², g³, g⁴} ∪ 8/7·{1, g, g², g³}.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Vum** | 1.0000 | 1 | 0.0 | 220.00 | 1/1 | **home / tonic** |
| 1 | Tol | 1.1429 | 8/7 | 231.2 | 251.43 | 8/7 (0.0 c) | supermajor second |
| 2 | Siri | 1.2236 | g | 349.4 | 269.19 | 11/9 (+2.0 c) | neutral third, the generator |
| 3 | Kun | 1.3984 | g·8/7 | 580.5 | 307.65 | 7/5 (−2.0 c) | septimal tritone |
| 4 | **Mor** | 1.4972 | g² | 698.7 | 329.39 | 3/2 (−3.2 c) | **pillar ("dominant")** |
| 5 | Ovit | 1.7111 | g²·8/7 | 929.9 | 376.44 | 12/7 (−3.2 c) | supermajor sixth |
| 6 | Hilu | 1.8320 | g³ | 1048.1 | 403.04 | 11/6 (−1.3 c) | neutral seventh |
| 7 | **Zish** | 2.0937 | g³·8/7 | 1279.3 | 460.61 | none within 20 c | **spicy: the colour note** |
| 8 | **Nimo** | 2.2416 | g⁴ | 1397.5 | 493.16 | 9/4 (−6.5 c) | major ninth; **leading tone** into the next Vum |
| 9 = 0′ | Vum′ | 2.4000 | 12/5 | 1515.6 | 528.00 | 12/5 (0.0 c) | period |

Names use no letters A–G. One period below the tonic, Nimo is 205.5 Hz, 118 c under Vum: that
is the leading tone the melody uses at every cadence.

#### 2. Step pattern and generator

- Pattern **LsLsLsLss** (4 L + 5 s), a moment of symmetry of 9 generators of 349.36 c inside
  a 1515.64 c period.
- **L = 231.2 c** (exactly 8/7), **s = 118.2 c** (≈ 15/14), **L/s = 1.956**.
- Degrees by generator count (mod period): Vum 0, Siri 1, Mor 2, Hilu 3, Nimo 4, then
  Tol 5, Kun 6, Ovit 7, Zish 8.
- Each generic interval comes in exactly two sizes (the MOS property), so every degree has a
  distinct position in the pattern, as in the diatonic scale.

#### 3. Intervals from the tonic, ranked by consonance

Primary criterion: proximity to a simple just ratio (n·d ≤ 100, within 8 c), simplest first.
Second column: Sethares roughness of two tones of the matched timbre at that interval,
relative to the unison (lower is smoother; asterisk = local minimum of the roughness curve).

| rank | degree | cents | just target | error | n·d | roughness (rel.) | mark |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Vum′ | 1515.6 | 12/5 | 0.0 c | 60 | **0.29*** | period; smoothest interval of all |
| 2 | **Mor** | 698.7 | 3/2 | −3.2 c | 6 | **3.5*** | **PILLAR** |
| 3 | Kun | 580.5 | 7/5 | −2.0 c | 35 | 6.4 | 12-TET misses this by 17.5 c |
| 4 | **Nimo** | 1397.5 | 9/4 | −6.5 c | 36 | 4.6 | **LEADING TONE** (one period down it is 118 c below Vum) |
| 5 | Tol | 231.2 | 8/7 | 0.0 c | 56 | 8.4 | 12-TET misses by 31 c |
| 6 | Hilu | 1048.1 | 11/6 | −1.3 c | 66 | 2.8 | 12-TET misses by 49 c |
| 7 | Ovit | 929.9 | 12/7 | −3.2 c | 84 | 5.4 | 12-TET misses by 33 c |
| 8 | Siri | 349.4 | 11/9 | +2.0 c | 99 | 6.0 | 12-TET misses by 49 c |
| 9 | **Zish** | 1279.3 | — | 20.7 c from 1300; 59 c from 13/6; 79 c from 2/1 | — | 2.4 | **SPICY** |

The three most consonant non-period intervals, 3/2, 7/5 and 9/4, are the chord-building
set used in the piece. Zish is low in roughness with the matched timbre (its partials fall
between the tonic's) yet near no simple ratio: smooth but *unplaceable*, which is what makes
it colour rather than clash.

#### 4. What it sounds like

Morsiri sounds like a major scale that has been left out in the sun. The first steps rise
confidently, but the third is neither happy nor sad, the kind of in-between you hear in
Arabic and Persian music, and the step above it is wider than you expect, so melodies seem
to lean forward. The fifth is where you expect it and feels like home away from home. Above
it the scale keeps climbing past where an octave should arrive, and never arrives; instead
there is one glowing, slightly unreal note (Zish) and then a long, satisfying slide upward,
semitone by semitone, into a tonic that sits a minor tenth above where you started. Phrases
end the way you want them to end, which is why it does not feel like an experiment. It
feels like a folk tune from a country that does not exist.

#### 5. Timbre: the matched spectrum

A harmonic instrument has a loud second partial at 2/1. Morsiri contains no 2/1, so a
harmonic instrument playing it would supply an octave the scale lacks and clash with Hilu
(1048 c) and Zish (1279 c). The piece therefore uses a spectrum built from the scale's own
frame: the period and the pillar, iterated.

| partial | ratio to fundamental | cents | amplitude | why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.0000 | 0 | 1.00 | fundamental |
| 2 | 1.4972 | 698.7 | 0.55 | the pillar Mor |
| 3 | 2.4000 | 1515.6 | 0.50 | the period |
| 4 | 3.5933 | 2214.4 | 0.35 | pillar + period |
| 5 | 5.7600 | 3031.3 | 0.22 | two periods |
| 6 | 8.6239 | 3730.0 | 0.12 | pillar + two periods |
| 7 | 13.8240 | 4546.9 | 0.06 | three periods |

Every partial lies on a scale degree in some period (Vum, Mor, Vum′, Mor′, Vum″, Mor″, Vum‴),
so two tones a pillar or a period apart share partials and do not beat. Upper partials fade
faster than the fundamental during each note, like a struck body. `timbre.py` computes the
Sethares roughness curve (b₁ = 3.5, b₂ = 5.75, s₁ = 0.0207, s₂ = 18.96) for two such tones at
220 Hz: the intrinsic roughness of a single tone is 0.011 (a 9-partial harmonic tone scores
0.014), and the curve has minima at 698 c (Mor), 818 c (period minus pillar, the interval
from Mor up to Vum′) and 1516 c (the period), where it drops to **0.29× the unison**. With a
harmonic timbre the same period interval is 2.6× the unison. That is the point of matching:
the instrument makes 12/5 the most restful interval, which is what lets it replace the
octave.

#### 6. Novelty argument

Generator 349.36 c, period 1515.64 c, MOS pattern LsLsLsLss (4L 5s), L/s = 1.956.

- **Not 12-EDO.** Seven of nine intervals are 15.6–49.4 c from any 12-TET pitch; both steps
  (231 c, 118 c) are absent from 12-EDO, and the period is 15.16 semitones.
- **Not 19-EDO.** 19-EDO does approximate 12/5 (24 steps, 0.15 c off), but the generator is
  5.53 steps of 19-EDO, so the scale's pitches fall between its grid points: worst deviation
  29.6 c.
- **Not 22-EDO.** 12/5 is 27.79 steps; worst deviation 24.7 c.
- **Not 31-EDO** (the closest relative, and the honest caveat). Because 31-EDO is a good
  11-limit temperament, every Morsiri pitch within one period lies within 6 c of a 31-EDO
  pitch (pattern 6-3-6-3-6-3-6-3-3 steps). But 12/5 is 39.15 steps of 31-EDO, not an integer:
  the two systems drift apart by 6.0 c per period and are 17.9 c apart by the third period
  used in the piece. A subset of an equal division repeats on its grid; Morsiri does not.
  Its L/s is 1.956, not 2.
- **Not Bohlen-Pierce.** BP is 13 equal divisions of 3/1 (or its 9-note Lambda MOS,
  generator 9/7). Morsiri's period is 386 c short of a tritave and its generator is 86 c
  from 9/7; worst deviation from the BP grid is 65.6 c.
- **Not Carlos alpha / beta / gamma.** Those are equal-step scales (78.0, 63.8, 35.1 c) with
  one step size; Morsiri has two. Worst deviations 37.4, 30.4 and 17.3 c (17.5 c is the
  maximum possible deviation from gamma's grid, i.e. no relationship).
- **Not Partch's 43.** Partch's Monophony is 2/1-periodic and strictly just. Morsiri is
  12/5-periodic and tempered (only 8/7 and 12/5 are exact). Over the three periods the piece
  uses it produces 27 distinct pitch classes; four of them are more than 8 c (up to 15.4 c)
  from any Partch pitch, and in any case none of Partch's modes has a 12/5 period.
- **Not a 3/2-generated scale.** The generator is a neutral third; 3/2 appears only as two
  generators. Octave-periodic neutral-third chains exist (Mohajira is 3L 4s in 2/1); the
  4L 5s pattern with L = 8/7 inside 12/5 is not a mode, rotation or subset of any of them,
  because a rotation or subset preserves the period and theirs is 2/1.

## Part 2 — The piece: `morsiri.scd`

**Language:** SuperCollider (tested with sclang/scsynth 3.13). Open the file in the IDE,
select all, evaluate. It boots the server, adds two SynthDefs and plays about 66 seconds.
Offline render without an audio device:

```
MORSIRI_RENDER=/path/to/out.wav sclang morsiri.scd
```

That also writes `out.wav.freqs.txt`, every frequency the score sends to the server, which
`verify_audio.py` compares against the table.

**How pitch works in the code.** `~ratios` is the nine-element ratio array built from the
generator. `~hz.(degree, periodShift)` = 220 × `~ratios[degree]` × 2.4^periodShift is the
only pitch source; every `\freq` in every pattern comes from it. There are no note names,
no `midinote`, no `midicps`.

**Rhythm.** Bars of 7 pulses (0.27 s each) grouped 3 + 2 + 2. A tick layer plays every
pulse: Vum one period down on the downbeat, Mor one period down on pulses 4 and 6, a faint
Vum on the rest, so the seven is always audible.

**Form** (33 bars, 62.4 s of events plus a 4 s release tail):

| section | bars | degrees | what happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| intro | 1–4 | ticks; chord Vum–Mor–Vum′; Nimo(−1) → Vum | the pulse alone, then the frame chord, then a single cadence as a promise |
| A | 5–12 | Vum, Tol, Siri, Kun, Mor, Ovit, Hilu; cadences Nimo(−1) → Vum | four two-bar phrases living on Vum and Mor, moving by step; pads Vum, Sept (1 : 7/5 : 12/5), Hilu, Siri, with Mor → Vum under every cadence |
| B | 13–20 | Mor, Ovit, Hilu, Nimo, Vum′, **Zish once** | the melody moves to the upper half of the period and cadences up into Vum′; bar 17 is the peak: Zish held three pulses over the tonic frame, resolved up by s to Nimo, then to Vum′ |
| A′ | 21–28 | as A, phrases 1–2 one period up | A returns a minor tenth higher for two phrases, then settles; each pad chord gains its root a period up |
| ending | 29–33 | Vum, Nimo(−1), Mor, Kun, Siri, Tol | the cadence slowed down twice, then Vum held over the frame chord across two periods |

**Harmony.** Chords use only the three most consonant intervals plus the period:
Vum frame 1 : 3/2 : 12/5; quintal chords 1 : 3/2 : 9/4 on Mor, Hilu and Siri (the Mor chord
contains Nimo, the leading tone, exactly as a dominant contains one); and the septimal
colour chord 1 : 7/5 : 12/5. Zish occurs once in the whole score (the frequency audit counts
it: one note-on at 460.61 Hz).

### Listening guide

- **0:00–0:08, intro.** Just the pulse: seven ticks, accented 1-4-6. At 0:04 the pad plays
  the frame chord; notice that it has no octave in it and still sounds at rest. At 0:07 the
  first cadence: a note a semitone-ish under the tonic rising into it.
- **0:08–0:23, A.** The tune. Listen for the third (Siri) that is neither major nor minor,
  and the stretched step up to it. Every second bar ends the same way: the pad changes to
  the pillar chord, the melody lands on the leading tone, and both resolve up into Vum.
  The third phrase starts on the pillar itself.
- **0:23–0:38, B.** The melody climbs into the upper part of the period and cadences into
  the *high* tonic, a minor tenth above the first one. **The surprise is at about 0:31:**
  a held note (Zish) that sits where no familiar note sits, three-quarters of a semitone
  above the octave that the scale never gives you. **The resolution follows immediately:**
  it slides up one small step to Nimo and one more to the high Vum, two semitone-sized
  steps upward in a row.
- **0:38–0:53, A′.** The opening tune again, lifted a period higher for two phrases, so the
  familiar shape glows; the pads are thicker. Then it comes back down to its original
  register and cadences as before.
- **0:53–1:06, ending.** The cadence in slow motion, twice, then the tonic alone over the
  frame chord spanning two periods. The last thing you hear is the sound of 12/5 being
  "home".

## Part 3 — Self-check (performed, with results)

All checks below were run, not assumed.

1. **Every frequency is derived from the table.** The offline render logs every `s_new`
   message. 548 note-ons were sent; all 548 equal 220 × ratio[degree] × 2.4^k for some
   degree and k ∈ {−1, 0, +1}, to within 0.01 c (`verify_audio.py`). Corroboration: a 0.19 s
   STFT of the rendered audio found 1063 strong peaks, 1024 within 12 c of a table frequency
   times a declared partial; the remaining 39 are all below 340 Hz where bin resolution is
   coarse and pad releases overlap.
2. **Step sizes alternate only between L and s.** `scale_search.mos_scale` rejects any
   generator/size whose step multiset has more than two distinct values; Morsiri's steps are
   231.17 c (×4) and 118.19 c (×5).
3. **MOS pattern is correct.** Sorting the nine stacked generators mod 1515.64 c gives
   0, 231.2, 349.4, 580.5, 698.7, 929.9, 1048.1, 1279.3, 1397.5 → LsLsLsLss. The same
   construction in `morsiri.scd` prints the same nine values (see the posted table when the
   file runs). L/s = 1.956 ∈ [1.4, 2.6]. The generator 349.4 c is 49.4 c from the nearest
   multiple of 100 and ≥ 36 c from 3/2, 4/3, 5/4, 6/5 (closest: 5/4 at 36.9 c).
4. **60 % novelty rule.** 7 of 9 intervals from the tonic (78 %) are more than 15 c from
   every 12-TET interval; the two exceptions are the 3/2 and 9/4, as intended.
5. **Four-consonance rule.** 8 intervals are within 8 c of a ratio with n·d ≤ 100
   (8/7, 11/9, 7/5, 3/2, 12/7, 11/6, 9/4, 12/5); 6 of them (8/7, 11/9, 7/5, 12/7, 11/6, 12/5)
   are ratios 12-TET misses by more than 15 c. Exactly one interval (Zish) is 20–40 c from
   anything familiar; none is further than 40 c.
6. **Synth partials match the period.** Partials are 1, r₄, P, r₄P, P², r₄P², P³ with
   r₄ = ratio of Mor and P = 12/5; each is a scale degree in some period. The roughness curve
   has local minima at Mor and at the period (0.29× unison).
7. **Code checks.** `grep` for note-name tokens (`[A-G][#b]?digit`), `midinote`, `\note`,
   `midicps`, `\degree`, `.midi` finds nothing. Every `\freq` is fed from `~hz`. The file was
   executed by sclang 3.13 in render mode with no errors or warnings (an undefined variable
   would have thrown), and the resulting audio is `morsiri.mp3`.

Two things were fixed during the checks: the first spectrum draft placed a 9/4 partial
118 c from the period partial, which made the instrument itself rough (intrinsic roughness
0.054); it was removed. And the first offline render failed because the SynthDefs sorted
after the first note; they are now placed at the head of the score.

## The song: "Where the Slow Moon Goes" (3:06)

A full song in Morsiri with a sung lead, built from the same tuning array. Listen:
`where_the_slow_moon_goes.mp3`. The lyric is an original in the manner of Hoagy Carmichael's
songbook, slow river, porch light, a pocket of yesterdays.

| file | role |
|---|---|
| `song.py` | the whole score: groove, chords, bass, arpeggio, melody, lyrics; validates and writes `song.json` |
| `morsiri_song.scd` | SuperCollider instruments; plays `song.json` live or renders `song_instrumental.wav` offline |
| `sing.py` | the singer: Piper voice + WORLD vocoder, every syllable forced onto a table frequency |
| `mix.py` | mix, room, levelling, limiter, mp3 |
| `verify_vocal.py` | measures the pitch actually sung and compares it with the table |

Build (SuperCollider 3.13, Python 3 with `numpy`, `pyworld`, `piper-tts`, and ffmpeg):

```
python3 song.py
MORSIRI_RENDER=song_instrumental.wav sclang morsiri_song.scd
# voice model (not in the repo, 120 MB): https://github.com/rhasspy/piper/releases/download/v0.0.2/voice-en-us-ryan-high.tar.gz -> voices/
python3 sing.py && python3 mix.py && python3 verify_vocal.py
```

**Form** (86 bars of 7 pulses at 0.30 s, 180.6 s plus tail):

| section | time | bars | what happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| intro | 0:00 | 4 | hats alone, kicks enter in bar 2, claps and the frame chord in bar 3 |
| verse 1 | 0:08 | 16 | four lines, each a four-bar phrase ending on the leading-tone cadence Nimo → Vum |
| chorus 1 | 0:42 | 12 | the melody moves up to live around Mor and Kun; the hook "of them is mine" rocks Vum–Nimo–Vum |
| verse 2 | 1:07 | 16 | as verse 1, new words |
| chorus 2 | 1:41 | 12 | |
| bridge | 2:06 | 8 | "it's the way the **light** falls": at 2:16 the voice holds Zish, the spicy degree, over the tonic frame, then steps up to Nimo and Vum |
| chorus 3 | 2:23 | 12 | a second voice joins a chord tone below; pads gain a voice one period up |
| outro | 2:48 | 6 | the last line returns, "where the slow moon goes", then the frame chord |

**Groove.** A fourteen-pulse cycle (two bars). Bar one accents 3 + 2 + 2; bar two answers with
kicks on 7, 10 and 12 and a clap on 13 as a pickup, so the cycle leans into its own downbeat.
Kick on 0, 3, 7, 10, 12; clap on 5, 9, 13; hats on every half pulse with accents on the kick
pattern. The kick is tuned: it drops from one period above its pitch down to it, not from an
octave. The bass plays the chord root on the kicks, the pillar above it on 9, and the next
chord's root on 13. A plucked arpeggio one period above the pads cycles chord tones on the
half pulses in the pattern root · — · third · fifth · — · third · root · — · fifth · — · third · root · fifth · —.

**Harmony.** Verse: Vum | Hilu | Siri | Mor→Vum, Vum | Sept | Hilu | Mor→Vum, Siri | Hilu | Vum |
Mor→Vum, Sept | Siri | Hilu | Mor→Vum. Chorus: Hilu | Siri | Hilu | Siri, Vum | Sept | Hilu |
Mor→Vum, Hilu | Siri | Mor | Vum. Bridge: Siri | Hilu | Siri | Siri→Mor | Vum | Mor→Vum | Vum |
Mor→Vum. Every chord is the frame 1 : 3/2 : 12/5, the septimal colour 1 : 7/5 : 12/5, or a quintal
triad 1 : 3/2 : 9/4 on Siri, Hilu or Mor. The Mor chord carries the leading tone, so each
cadence bar is four pulses of Mor and three of Vum.

**The voice.** There is no human singer in this repository; the vocal is synthesized. A neural
U.S.-English male speaking voice (Piper "ryan", trained on the RyanSpeech corpus, licence
CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, so non-commercial use only) is driven at the phoneme level, one lyric syllable
at a time. Each syllable is analysed with the WORLD vocoder, its vowel is stretched to the note
length (only the steady middle is stretched; the first and last 30 ms keep their natural
speed), and its pitch contour is replaced by the exact table frequency with vibrato (5.3 Hz,
11 c, fading in after 180 ms), portamento between syllables of a word and a small scoop at
phrase starts. The spectral envelope is kept, so it stays the same man. The harmony voice of the
final chorus is the same model with its formants shifted 6 % down. The voice's own overtones are
harmonic, as any human voice's are; the tuning rule applies to its pitches, which all come from
the table. The singing range is Mor one period down (137 Hz) to Mor (329 Hz), with the tonic
in the middle, like a baritone's comfortable tessitura.

**Verification (performed).** `song.py` checks that all 1822 pitched instrument and vocal events
are table frequencies (0 failures), that Zish occurs exactly once (in the voice, at 2:16) and
never in pads, plucks or bass, that the melody moves by step 176 times and by leap 20 times,
and that there are 25 leading-tone cadences. `verify_vocal.py` tracks the pitch of the rendered
vocal: all 216 sung notes measured, median error 0.6 c from the target, 90 % within 3.6 c, 99 %
within 15 c (the worst, 15 c, are one-pulse syllables that begin with a plosive). The SuperCollider
file ran under sclang 3.13 with no errors; the mix peaks at 0.97 with sections within 1.5 dB of
each other.
