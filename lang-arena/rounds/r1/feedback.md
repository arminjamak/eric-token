# Round 1 feedback (arbiter summary, held-out content withheld)

Winner: **B, Terse**, 87.28 points against A's Plain Loop at 77.86. B won on concision (39.1 vs 28.2) and was close on learnability (48.2 vs 49.7).

**Plain Loop (A):** decoding was almost perfect, both cold and with the spec. Its encodings were faithful and followed the rules. The cost was length: when no rule covered something, learners fell back on plain English, which makes encodings long. The comma and colon roles are slightly overloaded. A cold reader missed that each step in b5 waits for the previous one.

**Terse (B):** `lift` and `cut` are compact and decode well with the spec. Cold readers struggled with the chain of `cut`s in b8, but guessed it correctly, and they missed that the b10 factory is a *system containing subsystems*. In the held-out encodings, the learner wrote a delay without the number that `lag N` requires (rule validity 0.5). They also had no compact way to say "keeps getting worse", and only half-conveyed loop escalation. The learner found loops with mixed lift and cut links, recurring events ("each month") and threshold conditions the hardest parts to write.

Learnability is close to its ceiling for both languages. The largest remaining room is compression (B is at 0.78 of the maximum, A at 0.57) and encode validity on unseen systems.
